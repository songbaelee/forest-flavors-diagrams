Reading the market before the deal

Segmentation

Segmentation establishes the core constraint. Donors usually segment by value chain, but if you're a lender, the most useful variable, the one that tells you the most for the least effort, is the loan size a business is looking for.

Banks vs. funds

Most funds raise capital in developed countries, and that creates two problems. First, their cost structure is high relative to the markets they're investing in. Second, their capital is dollar-denominated, which only works well for export-oriented companies.

Pipeline

How many companies you need to see before you close a loan depends heavily on how that pipeline gets built.

Two funnels, one for an investment-led approach and one for a technical-assistance-led approach, that fill with dots representing companies and narrow from screening to evaluation to closed loans, with commentary above narrating each stage before switching from one funnel to the other. Green fill marks companies that receive technical assistance: in the investment approach, only the 10 closed loans after closing; in the technical assistance approach, all 600 companies before evaluation.

An average fund screens around 300 companies to build its pipeline. Of those, about 50 get evaluated in real depth. And roughly 10 actually close as loans. Technical assistance comes after the loan closes, and goes only to those 10. Now compare that to sourcing pipeline through an existing technical assistance activity.

A conservative estimate is that it takes twice as many companies to start — around 600. Here, technical assistance comes first: all 600 go through it before any are evaluated for a loan. About 100 of those get evaluated in depth. And the same 10 close as loans — twice the screening effort for the same result.
