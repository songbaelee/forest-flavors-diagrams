A combination approach to mobilizing capital

Forest Flavors, CRS Innovative Finance

This page lays out the range of relationships CRS could have with a lending partner. At the left are facilities CRS sponsors and runs itself. At the right are arrangements where CRS is one investor among several. The columns are clusters of real or proposed examples rather than sharp categories. We focus on the two middle columns, Mandate and Aligned, where CRS works through an outside partner but can still shape how the facility serves its programs.

Where partners sit: control to leverage

Our starting assumption is that we will mirror the technical assistance-first approach of Azure. In other words the core activity is the technical assistance provided by Forest Flavors, not the lending. Forest Flavors works with many SMEs and some of them also need financing.

It's also important to note that the technical assistance provided by Forest Flavors is to strengthen the SME overall not just to help the SME get financing. The goal is not to get 100% of the SMEs that Forest Flavors works with access to financing, but more likely a similar ratio as experienced by Azure (e.g., 300 SMEs receiving technical assistance and 40 receiving loans).

Three tensions to balance

We want to mobilize capital to support our existing programming but we also want to take a systems approach. The focus is on capital that can be deployed from social ventures (i.e., repaid) but grant capital can strengthen our approach.

  • Programs versus systems
  • Grants versus investment
  • Control versus scale

These tensions/trade-offs can be addressed and balanced through a combination approach which would reflect the market-system theme Julie and Shaun discussed, e.g. “from projects to systems”, “moving from direct implementation to a facilitation role”.

One option: Vula, Miarakap and Aceli

One such option to consider could be:

  • Vula is an AI-powered matchmaking platform for SMEs in Africa.
  • Miarakap is a fund manager and advisor in Madagascar that CRS has worked with in the past.
  • Aceli, a grant-based incentive activity, is raising a new wholesale debt facility in Uganda.

Grant/program funding

Vula is something that Jim is already in the process of contracting at the country/project level. This would be paid out of the programming budget as a pilot. It will provide CRS with segmentation data of the SMEs we work with and funding opportunities that best match their needs.

The envisioned relationship with Miarakap would mirror our past work with them when we were exploring an expansion of Isidro into East Africa. We would pay them to develop a pipeline that would include SMEs that CRS already works with, plus additional SMEs that we would jointly identify. The difference, though, would be that instead of building that pipeline for one specific funding opportunity (i.e., Isidro), we would market those SMEs to all existing capital providers in the ecosystem.

Investment/CSV opportunity

The new fund being raised by Aceli would typically fall in the fourth category (indirect), we placed it in the aligned category for several reasons.

First, Aceli is a marketplace approach. They currently work with 19 lending partners in Uganda, which span tier 1, 2, and 3 commercial banks, savings and credit cooperatives, and non-bank financial institutions. The SMEs that CRS works with are at different stages and have different needs, which cannot be served by one lending partner. Their focus on working with banks also sets them apart.

Second, Aceli's existing BDS technical assistance complements the type of technical assistance (TA) CRS typically provides, e.g., agroforestry, farmer training. There will be opportunities for coordinating two-way referrals for TA.

Third, their existing portfolio has already demonstrated overlap with CRS supported SMEs. Four SMEs that CRS works with in Uganda have already accessed financing through Aceli lending partners (worth over $1 million) and one has already benefited from their TA program. These results are roughly equivalent results to Isidro with no effort or cost from our side. The numbers would naturally increase with a directed and coordinated effort.

The Aceli debt facility would offer CRS a financial return of 2% to start, possibly 3-4% in the future. Balloon Ventures is a current partner / beneficiary of existing incentives and potential beneficiary of capital through the wholesale facility (Pearl Capital could be a partner in the future).

One drawback of this approach is that Aceli is in Uganda and Miarakap is in Madagascar. We focused on those two initial partners because of the strength of our relationships but other options are listed below that would enable geographic overlap from the start, e.g. I&P.

Travel

The annual CSAF convening was one of two events I went to every year when I was at USAID. The other was the annual convening for Aceli lending partners. The reason the CSAF convening was so valuable was both for the chance to meet with other attendees and also because of the content.

This year I think the trip would be even more valuable because participants include potential partners (Aceli, I&P, SIDI) and the topic this year is technical assistance (investment readiness, the landscape, scaling).

In addition, for the last several years, the CSAF convening has been combined with a one-day meeting for the Agri SME Learning Collective (ASLC) which is focused on accelerating the pace of learning in the agricultural SME sector and channel learning and best practices to influence practitioners.

Considerations and Other Options

Miarakap

While the initial focus of our work with Miarakap would be marketing the identified pipeline to other lenders, Social Ventures could always provide investment capital for Miarakap to manage on our behalf to make loans to SMEs that we think are highly impactful but are not able to access financing from other sources.

Single-country versus regional approach

Our suggestion to start with Miarakap is because of our existing relationship with them and the strength of our programming there but their work is limited to only Madagascar.

Similarly while Aceli hopes to expand their wholesale model to other countries, currently they are starting only in Uganda. Other Uganda-focused lenders include Balloon Ventures and Pearl Capital

Other options for partners on the advisory side that have broader geographic coverage include:

  • Agri Frontiers
    • Regional agri-SME accelerator that is raising a fund
    • Working with CRS on joint proposal for Forest Flavors (non-finance related)
  • I&P
    • Does advisory work (i.e., with donor finding), ecosystem building (they helped set up Miarakap), and fund management.
    • On the fund management side they have a donor-funded fund of funds called FASA that has provided a warehouse facility to Agri Frontiers.
      • Pearl Capital in Uganda (another past CRS partner) is in their pipeline.
    • They are in the process of raising a sidecar fund to FASA and are in talks with Livelihood Investment Fund as a potential investor

Options on the fund side that have broader geographic coverage include:

  • SIDI
    • French catholic NGO that provide financial services in the “Global South
    • Active in Madagascar with three outstanding loans in 2025
  • Global Innovation Fund (GIF)
    • Established by USAID (now defunct) and DFID (now FCDO)
    • GIF Core (the charity) is funded by donor grants
    • GIF Growth is a fund that relies on investment from governments or impact-oriented investors (recent funding round announcement). Built to leverage private sector investments and is designed to be self-sustaining long term.
  • Corus/Ground Up Investing (GUI)
    • Corus is formerly known as Lutheran World Relief (LWR)
    • GUI is setting up a new fund and raising third-party capital

We are in active conversations with all of the above. Concept notes and proposals from these partners are in the Reference materials on the contents page.
