Where partners sit: control to leverage

Forest Flavors, CRS Innovative Finance

This page lays out the range of relationships CRS could have with a lending partner. At the left are facilities CRS sponsors and runs itself. At the right are arrangements where CRS is one investor among several. The columns are clusters of real or proposed examples rather than sharp categories. We focus on the two middle columns, Mandate and Aligned, where CRS works through an outside partner but can still shape how the facility serves its programs.

We want to mobilize capital to support our existing programming but we also want to take a systems approach. The focus is on capital that can be deployed from social ventures (i.e., repaid) but grant capital can strengthen our approach.

  • Programs versus systems
  • Grants versus investment
  • Control versus scale

These tensions/trade-offs can be addressed and balanced through a combination approach which would reflect the market-system theme Julie and Shaun discussed, e.g. “from projects to systems”, “moving from direct implementation to a facilitation role”. One such option to consider could be:

  • Vula is an AI-powered matchmaking platform for SMEs in Africa.
  • Miarakap is a fund manager and advisor in Madagascar that CRS has worked with in the past.
  • Aceli, a grant-based incentive activity, is raising a new wholesale debt facility in Uganda.

Grant/program funding

Vula is something that Jim is already in the process of contracting at the country/project level. This would be paid out of the programming budget as a pilot. It will provide CRS with segmentation data of the SMEs we work with and funding opportunities that best match their needs.

The envisioned relationship with Miarakap would mirror our past work with them when we were exploring an expansion of Isidro into East Africa. We would pay them to develop a pipeline that would include SMEs that CRS already works with, plus additional SMEs that we would jointly identify. The difference, though, would be that instead of building that pipeline for one specific funding opportunity (i.e., Isidro), we would market those SMEs to all existing capital providers in the ecosystem.

Investment/CSV opportunity

The new fund being raised by Aceli would typically fall in the fourth category (indirect), we placed it in the aligned category for several reasons.

First, Aceli is a marketplace approach. They currently work with 19 lending partners in Uganda, which span tier 1, 2, and 3 commercial banks, savings and credit cooperatives, and non-bank financial institutions. The SMEs that CRS works with are at different stages and have different needs, which cannot be served by one lending partner. Their focus on working with banks also sets them apart.

Second, Aceli's existing BDS technical assistance complements the type of technical assistance CRS typically provides, e.g., agroforestry, farmer training.

Third, their existing portfolio has already demonstrated overlap with CRS supported SMEs. Four SMEs that CRS works with in Uganda have already accessed financing through Aceli lending partners (worth over $1 million) and one has already benefited from their TA program. These results are roughly equivalent results to Isidro with no effort or cost from our side. The numbers would naturally increase with a directed and coordinated effort.
